Alert evaluation

One alert per 60 minutes. A true alert must precede a new sustained high-glucose onset. The observed warning lead time is different from the 60-minute forecast horizon.

Limits of this evidence

The published data are interpolated. We retain cadence-compatible integer Dexcom values and delay model inputs by 15 minutes. Original device exports are still needed to confirm fully prospective performance.

A reproducible, partial Digital Twin

The model maintains recent glucose, activity, decaying meal states and delayed residual memory for each person. Static clinical context enters a histogram gradient-boosting model. Separate direct models forecast at 30, 60 and 120 minutes. This is a data-driven partial twin, not a validated whole-body physiological simulator.

Four disjoint participant partitions

What the alert means

A new episode is a crossing above 180 mg/dL followed by three consecutive five-minute bins above that threshold. The full episode must fit within the next 60 minutes. A positive result is a research flag for review, not an emergency diagnosis. The classifier score is uncalibrated and is never labeled as a clinical probability.

Calibration and missing data

Empirical 90% residual intervals are fitted on separate calibration patients. Their measured test coverage is shown in the Evidence view; nominal coverage is not a guarantee. Forecasts are suppressed after inadequate recent history or stale readings. Known interpolation and logging limitations are documented in the model card.
